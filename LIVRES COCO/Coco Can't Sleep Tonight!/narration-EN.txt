[INTRODUCTION TO THE READING PARENT]
Sleep is a universal phenomenon for all humans and animals. However, explaining what sleep is and why it is essential, even for adults, is not simple. This book aims to help children and parents learn about sleep in a fun and engaging way through the story of Coco, a curious little axolotl who sets off to explore the nighttime world.

Through encounters with different animals, Coco discovers that each one sleeps in a unique and fascinating way — and gradually understands why sleep is so important.

The principles described in this book are based on years of research in the field of children's sleep.

Dr. Anita NIRVENA

[PAGE 5]
That evening, it was bedtime. But Coco was not sleepy at all.

"Mommy, I can't fall asleep!" said Coco.
"Close your eyes and..." Mommy began.
"But Mommy, I don't have eyelids!" Coco replied.

Mommy laughed. "That's true! Then just stay still and sleep will come."

[PAGE 6]
But Coco couldn't stay still. Not tonight.

When Mommy and Daddy had fallen asleep, Coco quietly slipped out of the river and set off to explore the night.

"If I can't sleep, I'll find out what the other animals do at night!"

[PAGE 7 - ANIMAL 1/10] 🦉 The Owl
The first animal Coco met was an owl perched on a branch.

"Good evening, Owl! Why aren't you sleeping?"
The owl turned its head almost all the way around.
"Hoo hoo! I'm a nocturnal animal! I work at night and sleep during the day. But even I need to sleep. Everyone needs to sleep!"

Did you know?
Owls can turn their heads nearly 270 degrees! They sleep during the day, hidden in the trees.

[PAGE 8 - ANIMAL 2/10] 🐬 The Dolphin
Coco reached the edge of the ocean and spotted a dolphin swimming calmly.

"Good evening, Dolphin! You're not sleeping either?"
The dolphin made a little leap.
"Oh, I am sleeping right now! But I only put half my brain to sleep at a time. The other half stays awake so I can keep swimming and breathing!"

Coco's eyes went wide. "You sleep AND swim at the same time?"
"Sleep is so important that I've found a way to never go without it!"

Did you know?
Dolphins sleep with only one eye closed! The side of the brain that sleeps switches about every two hours.

[PAGE 9 - ANIMAL 3/10] 🦦 The Otter
A little further on, Coco found two otters floating on their backs, holding hands.

"Oh! Why are you holding hands?"
One otter opened an eye. "So we don't drift away from each other while we sleep! Feeling safe is very important for sleeping well."

Coco smiled. "That's like when I snuggle up to my plushie!"
"Exactly! Find what makes you feel safe, and sleep will come more easily."

Did you know?
Sea otters really do hold hands while sleeping! They also sometimes wrap themselves in seaweed so they don't drift away.

[PAGE 10 - ANIMAL 4/10] 🐨 The Koala
In a big eucalyptus tree, a koala was sound asleep, clinging to its branch.

"Koala? Koala! Are you sleeping?"
The koala yawned slowly. "Zzz... yes... I sleep... 22 hours a day..."
"22 HOURS?! But why so much?"
"Because my body needs a lot of energy to digest my leaves... That's what sleep does: it recharges your body with energy!"
And he fell right back asleep.

Did you know?
Koalas sleep up to 22 hours a day! That's because eucalyptus leaves are very hard to digest and give very little energy.

[PAGE 11 - ANIMAL 5/10] 🦇 The Bat
Under a bridge, Coco discovered bats hanging upside down.

"But... you sleep upside down?!"
A little bat opened its eyes. "Of course! That's how we're most comfortable. And while we sleep, our brain sorts through everything we learned tonight — where the best bugs are, which paths to avoid..."

"The brain works while you sleep?" Coco was amazed.
"Oh yes! It organizes all your memories and everything you've learned. That's why you feel smarter after a good night's sleep!"

Did you know?
During sleep, the brain sorts and organizes the day's memories. That's why you remember your lessons better after sleeping well!

[PAGE 12 - ANIMAL 6/10] 🐱 The Cat
Passing by a house, Coco spotted a cat curled up on a low wall.

"Good evening, Cat! Having a big sleep?"
The cat stretched its paws. "Me? I take lots of little sleeps. They're called naps! I sleep a bit, wake up, then sleep again..."

"And that's enough?"
"For me, yes! But for little axolotls, it's better to have one long sleep at night. During your deep sleep, your body repairs itself and makes what it needs to keep you healthy!"

Did you know?
Cats sleep between 12 and 16 hours a day! That's where the word "catnap" comes from.

[PAGE 13 - ANIMAL 7/10] 🐴 The Horse
In a meadow, a horse stood perfectly still under the moon.

"Horse? Are you... sleeping standing up?"
The horse half-opened an eye. "Yes! I can sleep standing up so I'm ready to run if there's danger. But it's a light sleep. To sleep really deeply and dream, I have to lie down."

"There are different ways of sleeping?"
"Oh yes! Sleep has different stages. First light, then deeper and deeper, and then comes the dreaming part! And it starts all over again throughout the night."

Did you know?
Horses only sleep deeply for 2 to 3 hours a day — and only when lying down! The rest of the time, they take short naps standing up.

[PAGE 14 - ANIMAL 8/10] 🦩 The Flamingo
At the edge of a pond, a flamingo was sleeping standing up... on just one leg!

"How do you not fall over?!"
The flamingo opened one eye. "Balance takes a well-rested body! When I sleep well, my muscles and body work perfectly. When I'm tired..."

The flamingo wobbled and just barely caught itself.
"...well, that's what happens!"

Coco laughed, but was starting to understand.

Did you know?
Flamingos often sleep on one leg! Scientists think it's to keep the other leg warm.

[PAGE 15 - ANIMAL 9/10] 🦥 The Sloth
Coco was starting to yawn. Looking up, Coco saw a sloth hanging from a branch.

"Sloth... I'm starting... to feel... a little... tired..."
The sloth smiled slowly. "Welcomeee... to the cluuuub... When you don't sleep enough... you get slow... grumpy... and everything is harder..."

Coco felt its legs getting heavy. "Is that... what's happening to me right now?"
"If you don't sleep... your body runs out of energy... and your brain gets all foggy... Go on... go to bed... little axolotl... zzz..."

Did you know?
Sloths sleep about 15 hours a day! Without enough sleep, our body feels heavy and our brain has trouble focusing.

[PAGE 16]
Coco let out an enormous yawn. Coco's gills were starting to turn pale.

Coco thought back to all the animals met that night:
The owl who sleeps during the day...
The dolphin who sleeps with half a brain...
The otters who hold hands...
The koala who recharges its energy...
The bat whose brain sorts memories...
The cat who repairs its body during deep sleep...
The horse and its different stages of sleep...
The flamingo who needs rest to keep its balance...
And the sloth who showed what happens when you don't sleep...

"Everyone sleeps... and everyone needs it... Me too!"

[PAGE 17 - ANIMAL 10/10] 🌊 Mommy Axolotl
Suddenly, a gentle voice rose in the night.

"Coco?"

It was Mommy Axolotl, waiting by the river.

"Mommy! I discovered how all the animals sleep! And I understand why it's important!"
Mommy smiled warmly. "And do you know how axolotls sleep?"
"No... how?"

[PAGE 18]
"We axolotls settle at the bottom of the water, in a quiet and dark place. We stay very still. And even though our eyes stay open... we sleep deeply!"

"That's why you told me to stay still!"
"Exactly, my little Coco."

Did you know?
Axolotls don't have eyelids! They sleep with their eyes wide open, resting at the bottom of the water. They prefer dark, quiet places.

[PAGE 19]
Mommy gently carried Coco to a cozy little spot at the bottom of the river and tucked Coco in comfortably with a favorite plush toy — a little stuffed fish.

"Mommy... sleep recharges your energy... organizes the brain... repairs the body... and helps you grow, right?"

"All of that at once, my Coco. And you know what else? We axolotls can regrow our legs and our tail! But for that, we need to sleep well."

"Wow! One more reason to sleep!"

Did you know?
Axolotls have a superpower: they can regenerate their legs, their tail, and even parts of their brain! Rest is essential for this extraordinary ability.

[PAGE 20]
Coco snuggled into the soft freshwater nest. Coco thought about all the animals around the world sleeping at this very moment — each in their own way, but all because sleep is magical.

Coco's gills turned pink and fluffy again.

And Coco fell asleep.

Good night, Coco.
